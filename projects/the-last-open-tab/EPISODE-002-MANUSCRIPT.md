# THE LAST OPEN TAB
## Episode 002: The Price of Perfect
### Original gothic-comedy play manuscript — draft 1.0

**Place:** The municipal night theatre. The gold line left by Episode 001 still crosses the rehearsal-room floor. A real ticket window faces the street. On the wall: a clock and a projector. Four playable voices: MARA, IVO, JUNE and THE PRESENTER. Ensemble ticket buyers may be shown by hands, footsteps and original recorded crowd sound. Approximate rehearsal target: 7–9 minutes. Actual duration must be timed.

**The dramatic promise:** The Presenter claims that if the theatre makes $10,000 by midnight, Mara will become perfect, the people she loves will stay together every day, and even their last nights will be painless. The play treats this as a claim made by a character, not a fact about money, death or an afterlife. Mara wants the money because the theatre needs it; she also wants the promised future badly enough to consider believing it.

### Scene 1 — The bargain

*[A neon slide snaps on: “$10,000 TODAY = PERFECT FOREVER.” A cheery three-note jingle plays. The clock reads 9:06 p.m. Under it, a handwritten notice says the landlord expects a deposit by morning. IVO is counting empty ticket envelopes. JUNE is setting out programs. MARA traces the gold line in the floor.]*

**PRESENTER:** Welcome back. Three hours until midnight. One number between you and a perfect ending.

**IVO:** If the number pays for this room, it may have a small point.

**MARA:** What ending?

**PRESENTER:** Ten thousand dollars in sales today. Then no more awful days. The ones you love stay. When your last night comes, you sleep without pain and wake into a happier story.

*[The slide adds an animated halo around MARA's head. She steps out of its circle.]*

**MARA:** You talk like a receipt can promise what no one can.

**PRESENTER:** Yet you looked at the clock.

**MARA:** I did.

**JUNE:** Is this a contract or a slide?

**PRESENTER:** Does it matter if the ending is beautiful?

**JUNE:** It matters before anybody signs.

*[A new line appears across the gold scar: “WHAT DOES TEN THOUSAND BUY?” The projector-door begins to open.]*

### Scene 2 — The easy sale

*[A single envelope slides under the door. IVO opens it. Inside: a one-page offer to “sponsor” the show for $10,000. The money would arrive at midnight if the theatre grants the buyer perpetual control of the archive and the right to cut any line from every future play.]*

**IVO:** The amount is right. The terms are an ambush wearing a tie.

**PRESENTER:** You asked to make the money today. Here is the path.

**MARA:** It buys the archive. The people whose words are in it never agreed.

**JUNE:** It also says “payment authorized.” Authorized is not captured. The theatre has not been paid a cent.

**PRESENTER:** You're being difficult about happiness.

**JUNE:** I am being exact about the price.

**IVO:** The landlord will not take an authorized adjective.

*[He sets the envelope beside the ledger, unsigned. The door stays open.]*

**MARA:** If we keep the room by selling the ending, we will make the same mistake as last night.

### Scene 3 — The work

*[A queue has formed at the real ticket window. The original play is about to open. IVO has priced a special one-night performance at $100 per seat; the notice states clearly what a ticket buys. The house has 100 seats. No claim of guaranteed happiness appears on the sign.]*

**IVO:** I set this price weeks ago. I thought we would be lucky to fill twenty seats.

**JUNE:** What does each ticket include?

**IVO:** Tonight's performance. A printed program. Access to the archive exhibit we actually have permission to show. Refund terms are on the back.

**MARA:** Then tell the truth before they pay.

*[They open the window. The play does not require filming 100 separate people. Instead, a bell rings on a few real transactions while the house gradually fills. Between moments, the clock moves. A practical montage: a hand receives a ticket; JUNE checks the seat count; IVO changes a sign from 42 to 76 to 99. Each number is a stage fiction, not a claim about the real series.]*

**PRESENTER:** Ninety-nine. You need one more. Say I can make them perfect. It fits on the poster.

**MARA:** The poster says what they get.

**PRESENTER:** Your happiness is waiting on a sentence.

**MARA:** My happiness isn't a feature we can sell to strangers.

*[The final buyer approaches. It is the MENTOR's old coat, worn by an ordinary audience member whose face we can see. No supernatural reveal.]*

**BUYER** *(may be played by PRESENTer with a clear costume and lighting change)*: Is there one seat left?

**IVO:** There is. One performance tonight. No promise it'll change your life.

**BUYER:** Good. I came for a play.

*[The buyer pays. JUNE sees the actual fictional point-of-sale confirmation, not a reservation or an unpaid invoice. She and IVO reconcile the 100 paid tickets with the seat count. She writes: “Gross paid ticket sales: 100 × $100 = $10,000. Refunds, fees, taxes and performance costs still need their own lines.”]*

### Scene 4 — What the number cannot do

*[The clock turns 11:59. The Presenter throws a blinding “PERFECT” slide over the full house. IVO cuts the projection. The house is not perfect: a chair squeaks, a program tears, someone coughs. MARA laughs, then almost cries.]*

**PRESENTER:** The number is real. You met my condition. Why won't you wear the halo?

**MARA:** Because your next claim never came with evidence.

**PRESENTER:** You wanted every day together. You wanted the last night to be kind.

**MARA:** I still do. Wanting it does not make you able to sell it.

**IVO:** We can pay the deposit if the available cash covers it. We still owe a show, refunds if due, and everyone who worked tonight.

**JUNE:** Ten thousand gross. A real achievement in this story. Not ten thousand profit. Not perfection. We will reconcile the final ledger after the performance.

**PRESENTER:** So what did the audience buy?

**MARA:** A night here. A play. A chance to leave with a question they can answer for themselves.

*[The gold line across the floor glows. The door opens once more, then shuts on the word “PERFECT.” A mark remains beside the old one: a small square the shape of a ticket. The house bell rings.]*

**IVO:** Places.

**MARA:** I am here.

**JUNE:** So am I.

*[They enter the light together. Blackout.]*

## Canon receipt, proposed until reviewed

**Local question resolved:** The theatre earns $10,000 in gross paid ticket sales **inside this fiction** without selling permanent control of the archive. The team rejects the Presenter's unsupported promise that a number can make a person perfect or guarantee a painless ending.

**Persistent change:** The theatre has a full house and an obligation to deliver its show; the gold floor line now carries a ticket-shaped mark. The Presenter has discovered Mara's deepest leverage: a promised future with people she loves. Mara has named that desire without selling it.

**Open questions:** Who sent the archive-control contract? Who wore the mentor's coat in Episode 001, and why? What will the real net cash be after costs? These are story questions, not evidence of a real buyer or payment.

**Cast note:** Mara's wish is sincere. Do not play her as foolish for wanting safety, love or rest. June's precision protects those wishes from exploitation. Ivo's financial pressure is also real. The Presenter is frightening because the offer blends a real need with an impossible guarantee.

**Director note:** Show the sale montage as labour: a queue, ticket count, delivery obligation. Keep the “PERFECT” slide alluring for one beat, then let imperfect human sounds reclaim the room. Avoid graphic death imagery. The ending values the people present, without claiming they will always be together.

**Producer note:** No real $10,000 sale is implied. Use original graphics, a fictitious payment interface and extras with consent. Do not film a real payment terminal, personal payment details or a false live revenue dashboard. The financial arithmetic is story logic, not an income method offered to viewers.
