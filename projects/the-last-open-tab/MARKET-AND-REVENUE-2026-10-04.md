## Comparative opportunities

**Strongest broad-attention references:** *Wednesday*, *Beetlejuice*, *Coraline* and *The Nightmare Before Christmas* are recognizable beyond narrow game audiences. Their advantage came from established IP, distribution, distinctive character/visual design and years of exposure. Treat them as craft comparators and proof that gothic storytelling can be culturally durable, not as a reachable revenue forecast for a new show.

**Strongest tonal matches to the user's request:** *Alice: Madness Returns* for unreliable, emotionally charged visual worlds; *Lollipop Chainsaw* for high-color camp against darkness; *What We Do in the Shadows* for character-based absurdity; *Little Nightmares* for visual readability. *The Last Open Tab* needs one clean throughline. Combining every signature feature would blur its identity and raise copying risk.

**Strongest indie production lessons:** *Little Nightmares*' visual clarity, stage tension in *Sweeney Todd*, and carefully limited locations can translate to a one-room pilot. A high-budget gothic castle or CGI monster is not required for the proposed projector-door mechanic.

**Seasonality hypothesis:** Gothic media may receive attention around Halloween, while character-led work can also travel year-round. We have not measured this seasonality for the chosen keywords or accounts. Since the user-facing date is October 4, 2026, a seasonal test is timely in principle, but the actual release would need a finished film, available accounts, rights and a publishing plan; do not rush an unfinished pilot to meet a calendar date.

**Current-market research gap:** To call any title “top ten now,” we need a consistent Google Trends snapshot (region, 12-month or 30-day window, YouTube Search or Web Search, exact search term/topic matching), current platform analytics and recent publisher/distributor sources. The ten names in `GOTHIC-RESEARCH.md` are editorial candidates. Neither a normalized Trends index nor a viral clip count proves people will buy *The Last Open Tab*. Source URLs and checked dates should be attached to any eventual market claim.
