# THE LAST OPEN TAB
## Episode 001: The Door in the Notes
### An original short play and screen pilot

**Draft:** 1.1, October 4, 2026 Pacific. Gothic comic stage revision after the user named game references. Written for four voices, one room, a portable projector or on-screen light effect, a chair, a table, paper notes and a stopped wall clock. Estimated full performance: 6–8 minutes, subject to rehearsal. No physical binding or forced restraint is needed. The “endless presentation” is performed through rhythm, light and the actors' choices.

**Cast**

- MARA VALE, projectionist, listening from the chair.
- IVO REED, theatre director, guarding the show clock.
- JUNE ARROYO, archivist, guarding the record.
- THE PRESENTER, a live performer or recorded voice. Their motive is to make the room accept a neat ending.

**Set:** An empty municipal theatre rehearsal room. On the table: a stack of numbered pages, a cup of water, a lamp and a projector remote. Downstage: a chair facing a white wall. The wall shows a too-cheerful acid-neon slide headed “CONCLUSION,” framed by dark theatre velvet. The clock has stopped at 11:59. A strip of tape on the floor marks a place where an unseen door might open.

**Before curtain:** A low projector fan, then a wildly upbeat three-note “finished!” chime that keeps arriving at the wrong moment. The cue is original and should be composed for this show. Nothing else. In the first beat, the audience sees the last slide of a presentation that seems to have gone on for hours. The performers never need to mime an actual multihour sequence.

---

### Scene 1: The last slide

*[The PRESENTATION click sounds. “CONCLUSION” appears again, and a gust from the projector lifts one corner of every page. IVO catches them with his show call sheet. MARA sits in the chair. Her notebook is full. She is free to stand, but the audience sees why she stays: the final slide might answer the question she came for.]*

**PRESENTER:** Conclusion. The mentor left at eleven fifty-nine. No one saw why. Therefore the theatre may close the file.

**IVO:** Good. We open in twenty minutes. Mara, lights. June, archive the notes. If the slide applauds itself again, I am billing it for a ticket.

**MARA:** Page twenty-seven is missing.

**IVO:** It is page twenty-eight.

**MARA:** Exactly. Our conclusion skipped a stair.

*[The PRESENTATION advances. A photograph of the empty theatre appears. Across the door in the photograph, light writes: “WHO FINISHED THIS STORY?”]*

**IVO:** Is that in your cue list?

**MARA:** No. I only asked about the page.

**JUNE:** Then someone else asked something too. These letters show up when two people mean different things by one object.

**IVO:** The object is a page. It means a page. We have sixteen minutes to prove me wrong.

**MARA:** To you it means an ending. To me it means we have not seen the whole account.

*[A narrow line of light falls across the tape on the floor. The wall now looks like a door.]*

**PRESENTER:** Conclusion. There is no door.

**JUNE:** That's one claim. It is not evidence.

**IVO:** We have a paying audience downstairs.

**MARA:** Then let's be on time with the truth.

*[She rises from the chair. The projector does not stop.]*

### Scene 2: The gap

*[JUNE lays the numbered pages on the table. IVO checks his watch. MARA points the projector at the wall, then at the paper. A blank rectangle sits between pages twenty-six and twenty-eight. Pages flutter across the tape line; MARA pins them down with the harmless paper remote. The neon “CONCLUSION” keeps glowing above the gap.]*

**JUNE:** We have twenty-six. We have twenty-eight. We do not have twenty-seven. The photograph in the slide is dated, but the original file is not here. I can prove the sequence is incomplete. I cannot prove why.

**IVO:** An incomplete sequence is not a conspiracy.

**JUNE:** Agreed.

**MARA:** Nor is it a conclusion.

**PRESENTER:** The clock stopped when the mentor left. A stopped clock is a time stamp.

**JUNE:** A stopped clock is a stopped clock.

*[MARA turns the lamp off. On the white wall, the projector replays the mentor entering the theatre. The figure turns toward someone out of frame. The picture freezes. There is no sound.]*

**MARA:** Show the next frame.

*[The projector advances to an empty stage.]*

**MARA:** There is a jump.

**IVO:** Could be a cut.

**JUNE:** Could be a fault in the machine. Could be an edit. We need the physical notes and the source file.

**MARA:** June, what is on page twenty-eight?

**JUNE:** “Everyone agreed that the mentor walked out.”

**MARA:** Did everyone agree?

**IVO:** I did. I was here. I saw the coat at the exit.

**JUNE:** You saw a coat at the exit. Did you see whose shoulders were inside it?

*[IVO looks at the photograph.]*

**IVO:** No.

**PRESENTER:** The audience will not enjoy uncertainty.

**IVO:** The audience deserves to know where we stand.

### Scene 3: The choice

*[The clock starts again: tick, tick. IVO picks up the show call sheet. The PREMIERE is now sixteen minutes away. A handwritten note falls from between two sheets.]*

**IVO:** This was in my folder.

**JUNE:** Read it aloud only if it is meant for the room.

**IVO:** It is addressed to all three of us. “If the picture skips, do not ask the machine to invent the missing seconds. Ask the people in the room what they did.” Signed, the mentor.

**MARA:** That's her handwriting.

**JUNE:** You recognize it. I will compare it with the archive before I call it confirmed. For now, we know the note was in Ivo's folder.

**PRESENTER:** It changes nothing. The slide is ready. The ending is ready.

**IVO:** The show is ready. That's different.

*[He takes the remote. He can play the polished slide or switch to a live introduction.]*

**IVO:** If I pull the ending, the audience may ask for a refund.

**MARA:** If you play it, they will believe we know what happened.

**JUNE:** We can tell them exactly what we know. The room was entered. The clock stopped. A coat was seen. The image skips. We have not verified the missing page or the author of the note.

**IVO:** Four facts and three holes. That's a difficult opening night.

**MARA:** It is an honest one.

*[IVO turns off the slide. For one beat, the wall is blank. The line of light on the floor becomes a door, then closes. A mark remains: a thin gold line in the wood.]*

**PRESENTER:** You have ruined my conclusion.

**MARA:** We finished this question. We didn't finish her story.

**JUNE:** Record it.

*[MARA writes in the notebook, speaking as she writes.]*

**MARA:** “We thought the slide proved the mentor left. We learned the record skips. Ivo saw a coat, not a face. We chose to tell the audience the limit. The theatre opens with the question intact.”

**IVO:** “Cost: one clean ending.”

**JUNE:** “Gain: a record someone else can challenge.”

*[The clock reads 12:00. A house bell rings. IVO opens the real rehearsal-room door. In the hall, someone has placed a coat with no name tag.]*

**IVO:** Mara. Is this the coat I saw?

*[MARA reaches for it, stops, and looks back at JUNE.]*

**MARA:** We can check.

*[Blackout.]*

### Canon receipt after episode

**Local resolution:** The team rejects the claim that the slide proves the mentor left. They agree on a truthful opening for the audience. The question door closes.

**Persistent change:** A gold line remains on the rehearsal-room floor; the theatre's opening statement changes. Ivo has publicly accepted uncertainty, affecting future budget and trust decisions.

**Open thread:** Whose coat was at the exit? The mentor's whereabouts remain unresolved. The note's handwriting is a character recognition, not yet archive-verified fact.

**Performer note (Mara):** I do not play a prisoner. I choose to stay because the last slide may answer a personal question. Standing up is an active choice. My last line is curious, not a cliffhanger gasp.

**Director note:** The projector's “proof” should look enticingly complete before the missing frame is exposed. Give the blank wall one full breath. Never show an image of a missing page as though it were evidence.

**Producer note:** One room, one hall edge and four voices are sufficient. Practical light and an original slide deck can replace expensive effects. Clear the rights for the coat photo and all displayed text; preferably film or draw them in-house. No generic trending keyword should be added to a line of dialogue simply for search traffic.
