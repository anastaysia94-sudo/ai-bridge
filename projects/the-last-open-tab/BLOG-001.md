# The Door in the Notes: an original short play about a missing page

*By SmartPickShop Holdings creative studio — draft for review, October 4, 2026 Pacific. Fiction. Film, publication and audience response pending.*

The presentation has reached its conclusion. The last slide says the mentor left the theatre at 11:59. Mara looks down at the notes and finds page twenty-six followed by page twenty-eight.

That gap is enough to change opening night.

In **The Last Open Tab**, unfinished questions appear as projections on ordinary objects. A projection can show an action, but it cannot read minds or recover missing time. When Mara notices the skipped page, the theatre director Ivo wants to proceed with the show. Archivist June asks what the team can actually prove. Their argument opens a door made of light across the rehearsal room floor.

The episode's question is simple: **Who decided the story was finished?**

## What happens in the play

Mara has sat through a long presentation because its final slide might tell her what happened to her missing mentor. Ivo has an audience waiting. June has a stack of notes that will become the public record. They all have a reason to care about the same page, but they do not mean the same thing by it.

The projector shows the mentor entering the theatre. Then the image jumps to an empty stage. Ivo says he saw a coat at the exit. June points out the limit: the footage does not show the missing seconds, and Ivo did not see the person's face. An apparent answer becomes a smaller, clearer question.

A note falls from Ivo's folder. It appears to be from the mentor, but June will not declare it authentic without comparing the handwriting to the archive. That delay matters. The characters can still act: Ivo turns off the polished ending and opens the show with what they know, what they do not know, and what they plan to check.

The projected door closes. A gold line remains in the floor. Their decision changed something. In the hall hangs a coat with no name tag. Mara's final line is not a claim that she knows whose coat it is: “We can check.”

## Why the episode is a play

The dramatic action happens in a room with a chair, clock, projector and papers. Each person wants something different now. Mara wants an answer about her mentor. Ivo wants a show he can open on time. June wants the public record to survive scrutiny. The Presenter wants one neat conclusion. No one needs a lecture about uncertainty; the actors must make choices under pressure.

One room and a few practical light cues keep the production feasible. A vertical short can focus on faces, page numbers and the remote; the landscape episode can hold the whole room and the space between people. The blog carries the story in text for readers who cannot or do not want to play video.

## Transcript excerpt

> **MARA:** Page twenty-seven is missing.
>
> **IVO:** It is page twenty-eight.
>
> **MARA:** Exactly.
>
> **JUNE:** I can prove the sequence is incomplete. I cannot prove why.
>
> **IVO:** If I pull the ending, the audience may ask for a refund.
>
> **MARA:** If you play it, they will believe we know what happened.
>
> **IVO:** Then we tell them what we know.
